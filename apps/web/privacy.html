Privacy & data.

Updated 6 September 2026

Atlas works with a local browser library. If you create an account and connect your extension, new captures also sync to your private cloud library. You choose when to connect and whether to import older captures.

What you save

Atlas captures screenshots, highlighted text, bookmarks, images, posts on X and notes when you use its capture actions. Saving a page can collect a readable copy of its useful text; headings; visited and canonical URLs; title and description; publisher and author; published and modified dates; language; lead image and favicon; capture and extraction timestamps; the capture method; extractor version; extraction status; and a content fingerprint. A saved link or image also records its target URL. Sites do not always provide every field.

Your local library

Captures are stored in IndexedDB in the browser profile where Atlas is installed. Extension connection settings and a short-lived cache of your capture preferences are stored in browser extension storage. You can save, browse and search captures without a companion. Without a connected account, captures stay in this local library. An account is optional. Existing local captures are not uploaded automatically when you connect.

Your optional cloud account

Creating an account stores your name, email address and a password hash on Atlas’s server. Your password and one-time recovery code are stored as hashes, rather than readable values. Browser login uses an HTTP-only session cookie. The extension receives a separate connection credential stored in Chrome’s extension storage.

After you connect a browser from the dashboard, new captures upload to Atlas when automatic sync is enabled. Uploaded content can include screenshots and images, selected or readable page text, notes, the source record described above and the processing choices active when you saved it. Pending uploads remain in the browser and retry when a connection is available. You can explicitly import older local captures from the extension. Cloud captures and preferences are scoped to your account.

The web dashboard uses your account to retrieve saved captures and images. It does not copy your browser’s local library unless you choose to import it. Cloud account limits are 1,000 captures and 200 MB; your account settings show current usage.

Cloud processing and optional companions

Atlas’s server can process cloud captures to extract text from images and derive summaries and tags. You can turn OCR, summaries and automatic tags on or off in your capture settings. The choices active when a capture is saved travel with that capture. Processing runs on stored content; it does not execute commands contained in captures or browse their source pages.

If you separately configure an enrichment companion for your local collection, Atlas can send capture content to that companion. A companion running on your computer may use a remote model provider. Review the companion and provider’s settings before enabling it for sensitive content.

Account access and recovery

Your recovery code is shown once when you create your account, recover access or change your password. Keep it somewhere private: someone with your email and recovery code can reset your password. Atlas does not send password-reset emails.

Account settings let you revoke individual connected browsers. Recovery and password changes revoke existing browser connections and other sessions. Revocation stops cloud access; it does not remotely erase captures already saved on a device. Logging out of the website does not disconnect extensions.

Your account stores one capture-preferences document used by connected browsers. It controls available capture methods, page extraction, note source attachment, popup layout, automatic sync and organization. The dashboard can ask an installed Atlas extension to refresh after you save changes; the website does not receive the extension credential.

Browser permissions

  • Page access, tabs and scripting: support capturing page content, selecting regions and showing Atlas’s on-page capture controls. Browser restrictions can prevent capture on protected pages.
  • Storage: keeps extension settings locally; the capture library uses IndexedDB.
  • Context menus and alarms: provide right-click capture actions and scheduled extension work, including retrying cloud uploads and processing pending captures when a companion is available.
  • Connection access: supports account pairing, authenticated cloud sync and communication with an optional configured enrichment companion.

Deleting saved items

Delete a capture in the web dashboard to remove it from your cloud library. Delete it in the extension’s local library to remove that local copy. These collections are separate; deleting one does not erase the other. Removing the extension also removes its local extension data. Content already sent to a separately configured companion or model provider follows that system’s own retention and deletion behavior.

Export and account deletion

Download your cloud library as a JSON export from account settings. Exports include saved text, source records, image data and capture preferences, and may contain private information. Deleting your account removes its active cloud captures, preferences and credentials and revokes its sessions and browser connections. Local copies on your devices remain until you delete them there.

Operational backups may retain earlier copies until those backups are replaced. Account deletion does not erase files you previously exported.
